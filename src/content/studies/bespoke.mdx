<BodySection label="Version 1 · Bespoke AI" heading="Pick what you like, and an artisan makes it.">
  The first Bespoke started with one line. A ring, for my engagement in Lake Tahoe, for my artsy fiancée. It made nine images and you X'd out the ones you didn't like. Between rounds it asked questions like "Do you not like silver?" and kept a list of what you wanted and didn't, which you could edit. Every answer went back into the prompt. When you found the one, you sent it out and artisans bid to make it.
</BodySection>

<Row label="What I was thinking" heading="React to images instead of writing a brief." src={insight} alt="Do you not like silver? over the ring grid, with I like this, idk and Not my vibe.">
  People know what they like when they see it, even when they can't describe it. So instead of writing a brief, you'd type one line and react to images. Every X and every answer sharpened the prompt until you found the one.
</Row>

<Figure src={artisan} alt="Bespoke AI for artisans on a laptop: I create unique rings for weddings for creative people." video="/media/bespoke/v1-artisan.mp4" caption="The artisan's side. Say what you make, pick a request and bid on it (0:27)" />

<Row label="Why it wouldn't work" heading="Artisans would be bidding on a picture." src={bid} alt="An artisan's bid of $400 on a Pink Garnite Flower Ring, generated by a buyer.">
  The images showed pieces no one could make at that price, or at all. An artisan would be bidding on a picture, not a spec. And the buyer hadn't committed anything, so an artisan had little reason to take the job.
</Row>

<BodySection label="Version 2 · Bespoke" heading="Start from artisans who already have a style.">
  The second Bespoke built for the other side. Artisans who already make high-end work list it, and buyers find it. Each piece gets an NFC tag before it's sold. Scanning the tag opens its digital passport, with the maker, the piece and every owner since. When it sells, ownership moves to the buyer through a smart contract, and a share of every resale goes back to the maker and earlier owners.
</BodySection>

<Figure src={flow} alt="Bespoke on three phones: a feed of clothes, ceramics and jewelry." video="/media/bespoke/v2-flow.mp4" caption="Find a piece, buy it, scan its tag, and it's in your wallet (0:42)" />

<Row label="What I was thinking" heading="Handmade becomes the luxury." src={value} alt="Value Proposition slide: digital passports in users' wallets, stress-free second-hand purchases, and artisans earning on every resale.">
  As more things get made by machines, handmade work becomes what people pay more for. Many artisans already have a style and a following. What they don't have is a way to prove a piece is theirs. An NFC tag on each piece gives it a passport. A smart contract sends the maker a share every time it resells.
</Row>

<Row label="Why it wouldn't work" heading="The resale loop came before the first sale." src={lifecycle} alt="A loop from artisan onboarding and RFID shipment through product listing, purchase, escrow, delivery and resale.">
  We built the resale loop before anyone had made a first sale. To buy a vase, a buyer had to understand wallets and digital twins. And the tag proves where the tag came from, not the piece. Someone still has to check each piece when it's made.
</Row>

<Row label="What we never asked" heading="We never picked the problem." src={summary} alt="Summary slide: from sustainable materials sourcing, to a marketplace with creative AI tools, to a marketplace for digitally authenticated goods.">
  We never decided whether the problem was artisans finding buyers or buyers not valuing handmade work. Those are two different products.

  Ceramics, shoes, clothing, paintings and furniture each sell, ship and price differently. We designed for all of them at once.

  I designed for how I thought people would buy in ten years and hoped they'd catch up. It had to work for how they buy today.
</Row>

<BodySection label="How I'd build it now" heading="One category, one problem, today's buyers.">
  I'd pick one category and one problem first. For example, ceramicists who sell through Instagram DMs and lose sales in the back-and-forth. I'd start with something they'd use this week, like a checkout link with a simple certificate of origin. The passport, resale royalties and wallet stay as the ten-year direction, and each gets added when buyers ask for it.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="What I'd keep" heading="The read on handmade.">
  The read on luxury still holds. One-of-one handmade work will matter more as more things are made by machines. Demand for transparency is rising too, like the EU mandate for digital passports. [The snippets you enjoyed most]
</BodySection>
