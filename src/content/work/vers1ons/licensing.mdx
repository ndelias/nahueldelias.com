<BodySection label="Problem" heading="A license is only worth something if everyone can trust it, and ours couldn't yet be trusted.">
  What a licensor set on a listing didn't always match what the system allowed, so a remix could reach a store the license didn't cover. Once a remix was remixed again, it got hard to tell who the original rights holders were and who should be paid. And setting up a listing, with its contributors, splits and terms, was easy to get wrong.

  That mattered for three reasons. A release that breaks its license is a legal problem for vers1ons. Original artists only list their songs if they know they'll be credited and paid down the chain. And the license is what we sell: if it isn't true, there's nothing to sell.
</BodySection>

<Tabs label="Three parts">
  <Tab id="listings">
    <SystemFigure label="How it works" spec={licensingListings} />

    <BodySection label="What I did" heading="I led the product decisions on licensing and built it with our engineering team.">
      I worked with legal on the agreement, with labels and artists on the terms they needed, and with our founders on the license model. Then we turned the agreement into data the platform runs on. Every purchase issues a rights grant that points back to the grant it came from, so rights and royalties resolve up the chain however deep it goes. The terms a buyer signs are frozen at purchase, and one check decides where a remix may release, the same in the API, the release wizard and the delivery worker.

      On the screens, I designed the listing flow so an artist can set contributors, splits, terms and the stores remixes may go to, then sign and publish, without being a lawyer.
    </BodySection>

    <Steps />

    <Comparisons />

    <BodySection label="Outcome" heading="Live in production, with labels and artists selling licenses under these terms.">
      Major labels, indie labels and established artists list songs on vers1ons. A remix of a remix still credits and pays the original artist, and only goes to the stores its license allows.
    </BodySection>
  </Tab>

  <Tab id="drops">
    <SystemFigure label="How it works" spec={licensingDrops} />

    <BodySection label="What I did" heading="I built Drops end to end: a remix contest where no one leaves as a loser.">
      Most remix contests run on a form and a Dropbox link. Stems leak, entries have no license, and the winning remix can't be released because nobody cleared the rights. In a drop, the license is the entry fee. The artist is paid for every license sold, and every producer leaves with a remix they're licensed to release to socials and stores, with no legal back-and-forth between teams.

      I wrote the spec, designed the pages and built the backend. Each entry is saved as its own work that points back to the original, and the purchase issues a real rights grant, so a winning remix can go straight to distribution. The windows to submit, judge and release are enforced on the server, at purchase, at submission and at release. On top of that come the lifecycle emails, from the invite to the winners, and a judging page where the host plays, stars and picks entries.
    </BodySection>

    <Steps />

    <Comparisons />

    <BodySection label="Outcome" heading="Shipped and working end to end, with the first drops being set up with artists now.">
      A drop goes from create, invite, buy and submit to judge, winners and release window, on the same licenses as every listing. The buzz of a contest, with the rights already cleared.
    </BodySection>
  </Tab>

  <Tab id="sheets">
    <SystemFigure label="How it works" spec={licensingSheets} />

    <BodySection label="What I did" heading="A satellite app that gets the paperwork done before a license exists.">
      Most co-written songs have no split sheet, and without one there's no clean license to sell. So I built sheets.vers1ons.com on my own, from nothing: anyone can create a split sheet, or bring in one they already have, with no account. When it's ready, it collects signatures and the authority of everyone who owns a share, and then hands the song to vers1ons to become a license agreement.
    </BodySection>

    <Steps />

    <Comparisons />

    <BodySection label="Outcome" heading="Live in production as the first vers1ons satellite app.">
      Anyone can make a split sheet at sheets.vers1ons.com for free. Paying gets it signed by everyone who owns a share, and a signed sheet comes into vers1ons ready to become a license. It's how artists who've never heard of us find the marketplace.
    </BodySection>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<BodySection label="Reliability" heading="Every signature binds to the exact text that was signed.">
  Sending a split sheet for signatures freezes a version of it, with a hash of the agreement and the document, and the server checks against that version when each person signs. Signing links are stored only as hashes. License terms are frozen at purchase, and a revoked grant stops a queued release from going live. Store scope was verified end to end across a three-deep remix chain.
</BodySection>
