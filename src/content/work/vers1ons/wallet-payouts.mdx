<BodySection label="Problem" heading="The rights followed the chain. The money didn't.">
  A license knew everyone who owned a share of a song, but when it sold, the money went to one artist as a lump sum, and they had to split it themselves. New artists were asked for bank details before they'd made anything, and many left right there. And when we wanted to give licenses away, a coupon couldn't do it: transferring rights takes a real transaction.

  None of that scales by hand as sales and remixes grow, and artists and labels only stay if they trust that everyone gets paid right.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="What I did" heading="I made the product and UX calls, and built platform credit myself.">
  With a teammate's payout groundwork in place, I made the call to pay every contributor their share directly, and designed how artists see it: the wallet, what they've earned, and withdrawing to their bank. I moved Stripe Connect out of sign-up, to the moment money actually moves, so people can start right away and connect once they're using the app.

  A contributor doesn't need an account to be owed money. When an uploader adds someone by email, that person is emailed that they have a share waiting. They can create a vers1ons account, or connect the one they already have, even under a different email, and claim it there.

  Platform credit was mine end to end: a balance kept on a ledger, so a giveaway is a real purchase that transfers rights, with credit and card together at checkout, and a reversal when a sale doesn't go through. The team wired up the Stripe API; I worked closely with it, and with how every payout and money check works behind the screens.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="Reliability" heading="Credit can't be spent twice, and a failed sale gives it back.">
  The balance is the sum of its ledger rows; nothing is edited in place. A sale paid entirely with credit has no card charge to wait for, so it settles as its own event, with a scheduled check behind it, and a spend that never becomes a sale is reversed in the same transaction that marks it failed.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="Outcome" heading="Live: every contributor is paid directly, and no one hits a bank form to sign up.">
  Artists see their balance and withdraw it, and every contributor gets their share without the artist doing the math. Someone added by email is told they have a share waiting, and can claim it with a new or existing account. Giveaway licenses run on platform credit, as real sales.
</BodySection>
