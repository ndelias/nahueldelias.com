<BodySection label="Problem" heading="Outside tools know nothing about the license chain, so credits come out wrong and the original artist drops off.">
  An artist who licenses a song on vers1ons and makes a remix needs to release it to Spotify, Apple and the rest. Doing that means codes (UPC/ISRC), clean metadata, contributor credits and splits that match the license, and a delivery that can fail anywhere in the middle.

  It's also how royalties get paid right. The splits and contributor details collected here are what let vers1ons pay everyone on the recording side down a chain of remixes, from the original to the sixth version and beyond.
</BodySection>

<Comparisons />

<BodySection label="Under the hood" heading="Refuse before writing.">
  Submitting a release runs every check before it touches the database: schema, ownership, beta access, drop timing, payout readiness, the license's store scope, storefront timing, duplicates, worker readiness. Then it writes the job, the rights grants and the dispatch envelope in one transaction.

  - **Hybrid partners.** One partner issues the codes and another delivers to stores, a deliberate choice: no registrant prefix of our own.
  - **Durable orchestration.** One durable job walks seven replay-safe steps: context, codes, release setup, staging audio and art (ffmpeg to WAV), assembling and hashing the metadata, delivery per route, then waiting up to seven days for the partner's confirmation.
  - **Live status, no polling.** Each step writes the job row and publishes through Redis to Socket.IO, and the wizard updates in place.
  - **Remix lineage.** Remixes of remixes accumulate contributors up the chain and inherit derivative titles correctly.
  - **Post-release edits** are classified as metadata-only or full update. ISWC is editable after release.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="Reliability" heading="245+ unit tests plus adversarial endpoint coverage.">
  Metrics, tracing and an ops playbook. Production canaries found real bugs, all fixed: a partner's dedupe conflict, a label and parental-advisory rejection, and codes that didn't issue when the release setup failed.
</BodySection>
