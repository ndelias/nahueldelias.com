<BodySection label="The problem" heading="Buying a license was harder than buying a song.">
  The price sat halfway down the listing, the royalties read like legal text, and checkout looked like a cart. A buyer who found a song on their phone had to switch to desktop to finish.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="What I did" heading="I designed and built the purchase flow, on desktop and on phones.">
  I redesigned the listing so the price and terms come first, and built checkout as one flow from the order summary to the signed agreement and payment. I took out the desktop-only gate and rebuilt every step for a phone. The team built the signing, payments and rights underneath.
</BodySection>

<SystemFigure label="How it works" spec={purchasing} />

<Row label="The listing" heading="Price and terms first, where buyers look." step={1}>
  I put the song on one side and the license on the other, with the price and Purchase at the top. A purchase bar stays with you as you scroll.
</Row>

<Row label="Royalties" heading="Royalties in one line. The detail is a tap away." step={2}>
  Royalties became one short line on the listing. The worked example opens in a dialog, so the page stays light and the detail is still there.
</Row>

<Row label="Under the hood · Checkout" heading="Signed first, then charged, in one flow." step={4}>
  I built checkout as one page. The payment is set up when the page loads, so the card form is ready the moment it appears. It appears once the agreement is filled in and you leave the signing fields, so it doesn't jump in on the first keystroke, and once it's shown it stays, so a card you've typed is never wiped. On Complete, the page checks the signature again and waits for it to be stored before anything is charged.
</Row>

<MobileDesign label="Under the hood · On a phone" intro="On a phone, one page asks you to read a legal PDF, accept seven statements, sign and pay. Five competing boxes became two sections that collapse, read and sign, then pay, after three attempts at a two-step sheet. One initials keystroke made the page jump 165px, and it's 0 now. Four hand-built bottom bars became one, and tap targets grew to 48px. I wrote 44 of this flow's commits, the third most, and 18 were for phones." />

<Row label="Under the hood · Paying" heading="Credit first, the card only for the rest." step={5}>
  Buyers can pay with platform credit, a card, or both. Checkout shows what the credit covers and what's left for the card before you pay, and when credit covers everything there's no card form at all. Credit is taken before the card is charged, and if taking it fails, the card isn't charged at all. If the card is declined, retrying doesn't take the credit twice. If the checkout dies before it completes, the exact credit goes back to the pool it came from.
</Row>

<Row label="Under the hood · The receipt" heading="A receipt that never waits on a file." step={8}>
  View Purchase used to open a whole page with no way back. I made it a small receipt over the song's page. The agreement on it is drawn in the browser from the license itself, so it shows as soon as the license loads instead of waiting for the signed PDF, which is made in the background after payment. If that file isn't stored yet, the download says so and asks you to try again. Next, the button should update itself when the file lands.
</Row>

<Row label="Reliability" heading="A license sold keeps its terms." step={6}>
  Each signed agreement keeps its own terms, so a later price change never touches a license already sold. When we opened buying to limited accounts, tests covered every closed route, so nothing opened by accident.
</Row>

<BodySection label="Outcome" heading="Buying a license now feels as simple as buying a song, on any screen.">
  The price, the terms and the royalties sit up front, checkout is easy to follow, and it works the same on a phone as on desktop. What you bought waits in your library.
</BodySection>
