<BodySection label="Problem" heading="The app looked like it was built by a different team every week.">
  vers1ons grew screen by screen. There were seven different status badges, a dozen hand-built play buttons and prices in three different greens. Orange and pink accents competed with the brand, and none of it said who we were. The app didn't feel cohesive, and it felt amateur.

  Fixes didn't stick either. A color changed in one place stayed wrong in ten others, and every new screen started from scratch. For a product asking labels and artists to trust it with their music, looking unfinished was a real cost.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="What I did" heading="I designed the system and moved the whole app onto it.">
  I set the visual direction. Dark only, warm black surfaces so cover art stands out, lavender for the brand and actions, and green only for money, so the price is always the thing you can find. Inter for words, Red Hat Mono for data like prices, BPM and dates.

  Underneath, I built the tokens and the shared components, like the play button, the price pill and one status badge in place of seven. I redesigned Discover on them, and moved the rest of the app across in five waves, from the shell and wizards to the player and the smaller apps around it. Then I made it hold. A design check in CI blocks off-system colors, layering and motion, and a mobile spec builds touch sizes into the components themselves.

  I designed or shaped most of the app's UI along the way, including the cards in use today. The team helped build them.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="Reliability" heading="The rules live in code, so they hold.">
  Colors, type, spacing, layering and motion all come from tokens, and every screen builds on the same shared components instead of copying them. A check in CI fails any change that uses an off-system color, a raw layering value or off-token motion. Each exception it allows is listed in the check with a reason. Every control has a visible focus ring from its component, and touch sizes on mobile come from the components too, so a new screen gets them for free.
</BodySection>

<BodySection label="Outcome" heading="vers1ons looks like one product, and it stays that way.">
  Every surface runs on the same system, from the app and its wizards to the landing page and the smaller apps around it. New screens are put together from parts instead of designed from scratch, and CI keeps them on brand without a reviewer. People noticed. Labels told us it felt like a product they could trust, artists said it looked really good, and investors came back saying everything looked a lot better.
</BodySection>
